Welcome!

Serin Baek

I’m a final-year MSci Pharmacology student at University College London, seeking a PhD position. My master’s research investigates how membrane voltage varies across cell types and between the apical and basolateral membranes of primary human nasal epithelial cells. I combine fluorescence lifetime imaging with computational modelling to study how individual cells contribute to the electrical behaviour of the airway epithelium. Ultimately, I want to understand how changes in these electrical properties contribute to airway disease.

My Journey

My interest in pharmacology began during my gap year, when I volunteered at a mobile clinic in Cambodia. Meeting patients living with conditions for which effective treatment was unavailable made me want to understand the mechanisms underlying disease and contribute to the development of new therapies. I applied to study pharmacology at UCL soon afterward.

When I returned to Cambodia a year later, I spent time in an oncology ward. I remember sitting in a waiting room filled with warm conversations and laughter, only to learn later how advanced many of the patients’ cancers already were. That contrast has stayed with me, prompting me to consider how earlier diagnosis and greater access to treatment might change patients’ lives. Back in London, volunteering with people experiencing homelessness made me increasingly aware that barriers to healthcare persist even in countries with advanced medical systems.

These experiences have shaped how I approach biomedical research. At Cambridge, I investigated biomarkers for the non-endoscopic detection of Barrett’s esophagus, motivated by the possibility of making early detection more accessible. At UCL, my research on airway epithelial cells has introduced me to the challenges of developing therapies for cystic fibrosis patients who remain underserved by existing treatments. I have become increasingly interested in understanding disease at the molecular level. The patients I met in Cambodia continue to remind me why I want to pursue research that ultimately makes a difference beyond the laboratory.
