Final-year MSci Pharmacology student at University College London

I’m seeking a PhD position. In my master’s research, I use fluorescence lifetime imaging and computational modelling to study how membrane voltage varies across cell types and between the apical and basolateral membranes of primary human nasal epithelial cells. My Journey

My interest in pharmacology began during my gap year, when I volunteered at a mobile clinic in Cambodia. Meeting patients who had few effective treatment options made me want to understand how diseases develop and how that knowledge could lead to new therapies. Soon afterwards, I applied to study pharmacology at UCL.

When I returned to Cambodia a year later, I spent time in an oncology ward. I remember sitting in a waiting room filled with conversation and laughter. Only later did I learn how advanced many of the patients’ cancers were. I wondered what earlier diagnosis and better access to treatment might have meant for them. Back in London, conversations with people experiencing homelessness showed me how difficult it can be to access care even when services are nearby.

At Cambridge, I investigated biomarkers for detecting Barrett’s oesophagus without an endoscopy, drawn to the possibility of making early detection more accessible. My work at UCL has made me curious about how changes in individual cells contribute to diseases such as cystic fibrosis. These projects have shaped two questions I want to pursue in research: how disease changes the way cells work, and how earlier detection can become more accessible.
