Serin Baek

I am a final year MSci Pharmacology student at University College London. My current master's project is on bioelectric profiling of airway epithelial cells, looking into how ion channel properties and gap junction coupling could be a potential target for gene therapy for cystic fibrosis patients.

My Journey

My path into pharmacology started in a mobile clinic in Cambodia, during a gap year where I was meant to think about what kind of person I want to be. Meeting patients living with conditions that had no treatment made the idea of studying disease mechanisms feel urgent, and I applied to UCL not long after. When I went back a year later and spent time in an oncology ward, I remember sitting in a waiting room full of quiet conversation and laughter, and only later learning how advanced many of those patients' cancers already were. That gap between how people were living in the moment and what they were facing has stayed with me, and it's a large part of why I care so much about early diagnosis and access to treatment, wherever in the world someone happens to be.

After returning to London, volunteering with homeless communities showed me the same pattern: the people most vulnerable to disease are often the ones with the least access to care. That realisation has shaped what I want to spend my career doing, working towards a world where a good treatment outcome doesn't depend on where you live or what you can afford. Driven by this vision, I have expanded my focus to rare diseases, and I am currently investigating CFTR replacement gene therapies for cystic fibrosis patients with mutations that produce no CFTR at all.
