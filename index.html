Serin Baek

I am a final year MSci Pharmacology student at University College London. My current master's project is on bioelectric profiling of airway epithelial cells, looking into how ion channel properties and gap junction coupling could be a potential target for gene therapy for cystic fibrosis patients.

My Journey

Volunteering at mobile clinics in Cambodia during my gap year exposed me to the realities of untreatable diseases, inspiring me to study pharmacology at UCL. Returning a year later to an oncology ward, I watched patients share laughter in the waiting room—only to realize many had visible, late-stage tumors and mere months to live. This stark contrast highlighted the urgent need for early cancer diagnostics and global healthcare equity, which drove my research at the Early Cancer Institute in Cambridge.

Back in London, my work with homeless communities revealed that severe healthcare inequality exists locally, too. I dream of a world where everyone has an equal opportunity to live a healthy life. Driven by this vision, I have expanded my focus to rare diseases, and I am currently investigating CFTR replacement gene therapies for cystic fibrosis patients with mutations that produce no CFTR at all.
